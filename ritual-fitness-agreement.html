4. Custom booking system

Contractor will build a custom class-booking system for Ritual Fitness Hawaii as part of the Project Fee. This replaces embedding a third-party scheduler such as Mindbody. The system in Scope is the following, and only the following:

  • Public class schedule on the site
  • Client accounts (sign up / log in)
  • Book a seat and cancel a booking, subject to rules Client configures
  • Class capacity so a full class cannot be overbooked
  • Products Client can sell: drop-in, packages/class packs, and an intro offer
  • Card payments through a processor Client owns and connects (typically Stripe). Contractor does not take possession of class revenue
  • Late-cancel / no-show rules Client configures (for example a 23-hour window)
  • Admin tools for Client to create classes, assign instructors, set capacity, and see who is booked
  • Email confirmation when a client books or cancels

Not in the booking build unless added in writing: waitlists, gift cards, memberships with freeze/hold logic, recurring membership billing beyond simple pack purchase, instructor payroll, SMS reminders, video streaming, or moving years of old Mindbody history into the new system.

Client will open and own the payment-processor account, complete identity verification, and accept that processor’s fees and payouts. Contractor wires the site to that account. Chargebacks and class-policy disputes are Client’s.

If a payment processor or email vendor changes its rules after launch, updating the integration is a Major Edit unless it is a simple settings change Contractor can do as a Minor Edit.

7. Edits after launch

After Launch, unpaid “until it is perfect” ends. Unused allowances do not roll over.

Minor Edit — 3 per calendar month

A Minor Edit is a small change on an existing page that does not add a page, add a feature, or rebuild a layout. Contractor reasonably expects to finish it in about thirty (30) minutes or less, including publish.

Examples: typo; phone, address, price, class time, instructor name; rewrite a headline or short paragraph; swap one image for another Client image of similar crop; update hours or a social URL; a small color/spacing tweak; fix a broken link or display glitch.

Not a Minor Edit: new page; new section that needs layout; page redesign; form or booking-logic change; new gallery or integration; graphics from scratch; batch image replacement across the site; anything that is a Major Edit.

Three Minor Edits per calendar month after Launch. Unused expire at month-end. Several tiny fixes in one request on the same page, doable in one short session, may count as one. Target turnaround: five (5) business days from a complete request.

Major Edit / big edit — same thing

One included per twelve-month period starting on the Launch Date. Each one after that is $100.00. Unused yearly Major Edits expire.

Examples of one “thing”: new section that needs layout; rebuild a page; a new page; change form or booking behavior; add a widget; multi-image gallery work; redesign a core page or refresh brand across pages; a booking feature that Section 4 does not list; anything over about thirty (30) minutes.

Several items in one ask are several Major Edits unless Contractor packages a quote. Work starts only after Client approves the count and price. The Parties confirm in writing when the year’s included Major Edit is being used.

Contractor classifies each request as Minor or Major. If Client disagrees, work on that request pauses until they agree in writing.

For thirty (30) days after Launch, Contractor repairs defects that are Contractor’s error in the accepted Scope, at no charge. A defect is not a new preference, new copy, new photo, or new feature.
